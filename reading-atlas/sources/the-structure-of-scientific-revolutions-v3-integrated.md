# The Structure of Scientific Revolutions — V3 integrated infographic sources

## Edition and structure

- Thomas S. Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (University of Chicago Press, first published 1962).
- The poster follows the University of Chicago Press 50th Anniversary Edition contents: Preface; Chapters I–XIII; Postscript—1969. Ian Hacking's introductory essay belongs to the anniversary edition and is not counted as one of Kuhn's thirteen chapters.
- Chapter sequence: history's role; route to normal science; nature of normal science; puzzle-solving; priority of paradigms; anomaly; crisis; response to crisis; nature and necessity of revolutions; changes of world view; invisibility; resolution; progress through revolutions.

## Content synthesis

The visual cycle — paradigm → puzzles → anomaly → crisis → rival paradigm → new normal — is a reader-facing synthesis of Kuhn's argument rather than a quotation or a rigid universal algorithm. It preserves two important qualifications:

1. Normal science is productive puzzle-solving made possible by shared commitments, not intellectual failure.
2. One anomaly does not automatically falsify a paradigm. Crisis develops when especially troublesome anomalies erode confidence and a viable rival framework becomes available.

The poster treats `paradigm` in both the broad sense of a community's shared commitments (later clarified as a disciplinary matrix) and the narrower sense of exemplary problem-solutions. `Incommensurability` is presented cautiously: standards, problems, and meanings may shift across paradigms, making some comparisons non-neutral; this does not mean that science is irrational or merely subjective.
