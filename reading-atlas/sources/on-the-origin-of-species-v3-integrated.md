# On the Origin of Species — V3 integrated infographic notes

- Work: Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species*, first edition (London: John Murray, 1859).
- Structure: an introduction, fourteen numbered chapters (I–XIV), and an index. The infographic groups the chapters as I–IV (variation and selection), V–VIII (laws/conditions of variation, difficulties, instinct, hybridism), and IX–XIV (geological record, succession, geographic distribution, affinities/morphology/embryology/rudimentary organs, recapitulation and conclusion). The grouped captions are thematic summaries, not replacement chapter titles.
- Core argument: heritable variation exists among organisms; more individuals are born than can leave descendants; differential survival and reproduction can accumulate advantageous variations over generations, yielding adaptation and branching descent.
- Scope note: this is Darwin’s pre-Mendelian first-edition argument. The infographic avoids attributing modern genetics to Darwin and avoids equating fitness with physical strength.
- Evidence strands represented: domestication and selection, fossils/geological succession (including gaps), geographic distribution, comparative anatomy and embryology, and common descent.
- Original-language and Korean title in the image: *On the Origin of Species* / 「종의 기원」.
