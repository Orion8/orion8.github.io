## Verified structure

Oxford Academic lists an introduction, three parts, ten chapters, and two technical appendices:

1. **Part I — The Power of Prediction:** “Prediction Machines”; “Adjusting the Volume (Noise, Signal, Attention)”; “The Imaginarium.”
2. **Part II — Embodying Prediction:** “Prediction-Action Machines”; “Precision Engineering: Sculpting the Flow”; “Beyond Fantasy”; “Expecting Ourselves (Creeping Up On Consciousness).”
3. **Part III — Scaffolding Prediction:** “The Lazy Predictive Brain”; “Being Human”; “Conclusions: The Future of Prediction.”
4. Appendices: “Bare Bayes” and “The Free-Energy Formulation.”

## Content decisions

- The central model is hierarchical predictive processing: higher levels send predictions downward; mismatches travel upward as prediction errors.
- Precision weighting expresses estimated reliability. It helps explain why attention changes which prediction errors dominate learning and perception.
- Action is presented as part of the same loop: embodied agents can reduce error by changing their models or by acting to change the sensory stream, including through proprioceptive prediction.
- The brain–body–world diagram follows Clark's attempt to connect predictive processing with embodied and situated cognition.
- The poster explicitly labels predictive processing as an influential but still debated and testable framework. It does not present the theory as settled consensus.
