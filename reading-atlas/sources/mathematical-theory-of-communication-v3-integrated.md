# The Mathematical Theory of Communication — V3 integrated infographic notes

## Bibliographic and content check
- The 1949 volume by Claude E. Shannon and Warren Weaver republished Shannon's communication-theory work as a book, with Weaver's framing text. University of Illinois Press describes the theory's earlier publication as a paper and its subsequent book republication.
- Shannon's paper appeared in *The Bell System Technical Journal* 27 (July and October 1948), pages 379–423 and 623–656. The first-edition text begins with the communication problem and a five-part source–transmitter–channel–receiver–destination model.
- Shannon's paper is organized into four parts and sections 1–27: I. Discrete Noiseless Systems (1–10); II. The Discrete Channel with Noise (11–17); III. Mathematical Preliminaries (18–23); IV. The Continuous Channel (24–27).
- Weaver's introduction distinguishes technical accuracy, semantic interpretation, and effectiveness on the receiver. These are framing levels; Shannon's mathematical measure quantifies the engineering communication problem, not the meaning of a message.
- Central tools presented: entropy as uncertainty (in bits for base 2); coding/redundancy; channel capacity. The noisy-channel theorem is summarized carefully: in Shannon's model rates below capacity can be made arbitrarily reliable; rates above capacity cannot be reliably transmitted.
