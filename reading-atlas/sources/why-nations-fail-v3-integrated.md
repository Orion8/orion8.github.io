## Core model

Political institutions and the distribution of political power shape economic institutions. Economic institutions determine incentives and constraints, influencing investment, innovation, participation, and long-run development. Institutions are social choices contested by groups with different interests.

### Inclusive institutions

- Political power is broadly distributed and sufficiently centralized to enforce rules.
- Economic institutions provide secure rights, impartial law, public services, open opportunity, and room for entry and innovation.
- They can create a virtuous circle that makes later capture harder.

### Extractive institutions

- Political power is concentrated in a narrow elite.
- Economic institutions transfer resources and opportunity toward that elite.
- They may produce growth for a time, but elites often block creative destruction and the system remains unstable or limited.
- They can reproduce themselves through a vicious circle.

### Historical dynamics

- Geography, culture, and ignorance are rejected as sufficient general explanations.
- Small institutional differences can be amplified at critical junctures.
- Institutional drift sends otherwise similar societies onto diverging paths.
- Change is possible, but there is no simple universal policy recipe.
