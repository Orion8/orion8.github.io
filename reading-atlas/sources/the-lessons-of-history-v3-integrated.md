# The Lessons of History — V3 integrated infographic

## Bibliographic baseline

- Will Durant and Ariel Durant, *The Lessons of History*, first published 1968.
- The poster treats the book as a concise interpretive synthesis drawn from the Durants’ multi-volume *The Story of Civilization*, not as a source of deterministic laws.

## Verified structure

The 13 chapters are: “Hesitations”; “History and the Earth”; “Biology and History”; “Race and History”; “Character and History”; “Morals and History”; “Religion and History”; “Economics and History”; “Socialism and History”; “Government and History”; “History and War”; “Growth and Decay”; and “Is Progress Real?”

## Editorial synthesis used in the infographic

- The authors compare recurring pressures—geography, population, technology, wealth, belief, and power—across eras.
- Their recurring tensions are presented as interpretive tendencies rather than predictive or deterministic cycles.
- The poster condenses the book’s argument into continuity in human motives, changing moral and political institutions, repeated concentration of wealth and power, and cumulative cultural inheritance.
- “Progress” is framed primarily as the preservation and transmission of knowledge, rather than guaranteed moral improvement.

## Critical-reading note

Several claims about race, biology, gender, and civilization are historically situated in 1968 and remain disputed. The infographic therefore asks readers to distinguish evidence from analogy and to notice voices underrepresented in the Durants’ synthesis, including women, colonized peoples, and non-Western perspectives.
