# Six Easy Pieces — V3 integrated infographic

## Bibliographic baseline

- Richard P. Feynman, with Robert B. Leighton and Matthew Sands, *Six Easy Pieces: Essentials of Physics Explained by Its Most Brilliant Teacher*.
- The six selections come from Feynman’s 1961–1963 Caltech lectures and were first collected as *Six Easy Pieces* in 1995.

## Verified six-chapter structure

1. Atoms in Motion
2. Basic Physics
3. The Relation of Physics to Other Sciences
4. Conservation of Energy
5. The Theory of Gravitation
6. Quantum Behavior

## Editorial synthesis used in the infographic

- Atomic hypothesis: matter is composed of atoms in continual motion, with distance-dependent attraction and short-range repulsion.
- The book searches for compact physical laws while recognizing that higher-level sciences retain their own explanatory structures.
- Energy conservation is presented as an abstract but exact accounting principle across changing forms.
- Gravitation connects terrestrial falling and celestial orbits through an inverse-square relation.
- Quantum behavior is represented by single-event uncertainty and statistically precise interference patterns across repeated trials; no role is assigned to human consciousness.
- The unifying reading method is model building: imagine, calculate, measure, revise.

## Historical-science note

The lectures reflect the terminology and particle picture of the early 1960s. The infographic emphasizes the enduring reasoning methods and clearly avoids treating every period-specific classification as the current Standard Model.
