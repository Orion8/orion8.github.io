## Verified structure

The infographic follows the book's ten-chapter arc: measurement and scale; scaling as a common language; the unity and complexity of life; growth, aging and death; the shift from the Anthropocene to the Urbanocene; two chapters developing a science of cities; consequences and predictions for mobility, pace, connectivity, diversity, metabolism and growth; a science of companies; and a unified sustainability question.

## Core model used in the graphic

The central relation is `Y = Y0 N^β`, shown on a log–log axis. Biological quantities commonly follow quarter-power families: metabolic rate scales approximately as mass to the `3/4`, characteristic rates such as heart rate as mass to `-1/4`, and characteristic times such as lifespan as mass to `1/4`. City infrastructure is typically sublinear while socioeconomic outputs are superlinear; doubling city population therefore yields roughly 15% savings per person in infrastructure and roughly 15% increases per person in interaction-driven activity. The company section presents West's contrast between finite, efficiency-bound organizations and the more open networks of cities, without treating a statistical average as a destiny for every case.
