## Structure verified

The book grew out of the 1972 BBC television series, which comprised four episodes. The book contains seven numbered essays: essays 1, 3, 5, and 7 combine text and images; essays 2, 4, and 6 are pictorial essays intended to work through juxtaposition rather than explanatory prose.

## Core claims represented

1. Seeing is shaped by knowledge, context, and the viewer's position.
2. Mechanical and broadcast reproduction detach works from their original settings and place them in new contexts; framing and captions participate in meaning.
3. Conventions of looking, especially in the European nude, encode asymmetries between observer and observed. The poster marks Berger's historically binary framing as something to read critically today.
4. European oil painting, roughly 1500–1900, often made property, status, and rare things appear available to possession.
5. Publicity borrows the authority and visual language of oil painting, addressing a spectator-buyer through glamour, envy, and a promised future self.
6. The image essays invite attention to repetition, contrast, scale, sequence, and juxtaposition.
