# The Bible — V3 integrated bilingual anatomy

Prepared 2026-10-20 (America/Los_Angeles). Catalogue id 15; production sequence 21. Catalogue title The Bible is retained; Korean title 성경. A multi-author anthology formed over centuries, not a book with one author, publication year or uniform argument.

## Sources and scope

- https://bible.usccb.org/bible — primary publisher's canonical contents; Old/New Testament groupings and books, including Catholic deuterocanonical books.
- https://www.usccb.org/faq — indexed official explanation: Protestant Old Testament 39, Catholic 46, both New Testaments 27. Direct retrieval returned HTTP 403; the official indexed answer was available. Sums 66 and 73 are explicitly qualified by tradition.
- https://www.biblesociety.org.uk/uploads/content/shop/files/9780564046072_Sources-of-Wisdom-edexcel-look-inside.pdf — publisher education excerpt on two testaments, genres, varying Christian canons and interpretations. Its broad claims about authorship are not copied into this map.
- https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Tanakh — Jewish primary-text library: Torah, Prophets, Writings; an independent Jewish canon, not a Christian Bible missing its ending.
- https://bible.usccb.org/bible/genesis/0 — primary publisher introduction to Genesis, creation and covenant themes.
- https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/21 — primary text on the new heaven/earth and renewed city. The narrative panel is a labeled Christian reading frame, not an assertion that every text has one identical plot or that Jewish interpretation is superseded.

## Content boundaries

The map distinguishes structural contents from a reader's thematic synthesis. Law, narrative, wisdom/poetry and prophecy are broad browsing groups, not mutually exclusive genres. New Testament groups are Gospels, Acts, Letters and Revelation. Christian book lists differ; Orthodox variation is stated without imposing one count. No universal claim of 66 books is made. The Tanakh is presented separately and does not include the New Testament.

The illustrated arc (creation/covenant; liberation/exile/return; Jesus/early community; renewed creation) compresses a Christian whole-Bible reading. It does not provide a chronology of textual composition or historical verification of narrated events. The caption explicitly preserves the diversity of individual texts and Jewish readings. Source traditions make theological claims; this educational map describes their literary themes without presenting confessional assertions as empirically established facts. Manuscript marks and ancient scenes are illustrative, not archaeological reconstructions or quotations.
