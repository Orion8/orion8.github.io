## Verified structure

The book contains an introduction, 17 numbered chapters, and an epilogue. Its explanatory method starts with a behavior and moves backward through nested timescales: neural events a second before, sensory context over seconds to minutes, hormones over hours to days, plasticity over days to months, adolescence, early development and the womb, genes, culture, and evolution. Chapters 11–17 then apply this multi-level account to us/them boundaries, hierarchy, morality, empathy, lethal metaphors, criminal justice and free will, and war and peace.

## Core claims represented in the infographic

- A behavior never has one explanatory cause; a defensible account must connect brain, body, development, genes, environment, culture, and evolutionary history.
- The same physical act can be cruel or compassionate depending on context and intent.
- Biology is not a fixed destiny: genes are regulated by environments, brains change with experience, and culture changes what cues mean.
- Amygdala, prefrontal cortex, dopamine, stress hormones, and related systems are shown as interacting contributors, not one-to-one "aggression centers" or moral switches.
- The book's second movement tests the framework on group boundaries, power, moral judgment, empathy, justice, and collective violence or cooperation.

## Visual logic

The composition is a vertical time-depth cross-section built around one ambiguous human action. A central action marker connects to eight backward-looking timescale bands. The lower half turns the same causal stack outward into social domains. Illustrations, arrows, section numbers, labels, and prose share one editorial grid; no HTML text, external font, SVG text, or language-switch overlay is used in the published raster.
