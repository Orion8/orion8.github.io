# *Братья Карамазовы · The Brothers Karamazov* — source notes

## Edition and structure

- Dostoevsky's final novel was published in book form in 1880.
- The poster preserves the novel's actual architecture: four parts containing twelve books, followed by a three-chapter epilogue.
- Chapter counts checked from the Constance Garnett contents are: Part I, 24; Part II, 17; Part III, 21; Part IV, 31; Epilogue, 3. Total: 96 chapters including the epilogue.
- The five structural movements on the poster are interpretive summaries, not replacement book titles.

## Content and interpretation checks

- Oxford University Press describes the work as both a crime story and a philosophical debate; the poster keeps those two layers visible at once.
- Penguin Classics identifies Mitya with sensual passion, Ivan with intellectual torment, Alyosha with spiritual repair, and Smerdyakov as the shadowy probable half-brother. The poster treats these as reading lenses rather than exhaustive labels.
- Norton identifies evil, free will, and faith as central preoccupations. The poster expands these into paired tensions: faith/rebellion, freedom/security, law/moral guilt, and suffering/transformation.
- Book V's "Rebellion" and "The Grand Inquisitor" are placed opposite Book VI's Zosima material to show the novel's argument by juxtaposed voices.
- The legal question of who performs the murder is kept separate from the novel's broader inquiry into desire, influence, omission, shared guilt, and responsibility.
- Smerdyakov is called a probable half-brother, not an unquestionably established son.
- The poster does not claim that suffering automatically redeems; it can destroy or transform, and the novel does not reduce this tension to a formula.
