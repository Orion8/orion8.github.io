# 1491 — V3 integrated infographic source notes

## Bibliographic baseline

- Charles C. Mann, *1491: New Revelations of the Americas Before Columbus* (Knopf, 2005).
- The 2006 Vintage second edition is 576 pages. The original hardcover was published August 9, 2005.

## Verified structure

The Library of Congress table of contents confirms the introduction, “Holmberg's Mistake,” followed by three parts:

1. **Numbers from Nowhere** — chapters 1–4, on population estimates, epidemics, New England, and Tawantinsuyu.
2. **Very Old Bones** — chapters 5–7, on the peopling of the Americas, Clovis/pre-Clovis debates, maize, and Mesoamerican innovation.
3. **Landscape with Figures** — chapters 8–10, on cities, earthworks, Amazonia, anthropogenic soils, and managed landscapes.

## Argument represented

The poster presents Mann's synthesis as a revision of three common older claims: sparse population, a single recent arrival, and an essentially pristine wilderness. It shows how archaeology, demography, epidemiology, genetics, ecology, and soil science support a more populous, temporally deep, socially complex, and actively managed pre-contact hemisphere. Because estimates and interpretations vary, the poster explicitly states that evidence differs by region and asks readers to distinguish established findings from contested claims.

## Visual and language decisions

- Separate self-contained Korean and English 1024×1536 WebP files.
- Five integrated sections with illustrations, text, labels, and arrows composed together.
- Regional examples: Tenochtitlan, Tawantinsuyu, Cahokia/Mississippi Valley, and Amazonia.
- No HTML text overlay, SVG text dependency, language-swap layer, speech bubbles, outlined text, or capsule-shaped body copy.
- Korean and English were typeset independently; both retain the original title on their language-specific cards.
