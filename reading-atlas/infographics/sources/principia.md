# *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* — source notes

## Edition and structure

- First published in 1687; Newton issued revised editions in 1713 and 1726.
- The poster follows the final authorial third edition (1726), using Andrew Motte's 1729 English translation for the English wording and table of contents.
- Opening apparatus: eight definitions, three laws of motion, and corollaries.
- Book I, *De Motu Corporum*: 14 sections on idealized motion and centripetal-force problems.
- Book II, *De Motu Corporum*: 9 sections on resisted motion, fluids, sound, and vortex mechanics.
- Book III, *De Mundi Systemate*: rules of reasoning, phenomena, propositions, and the General Scholium.

## Content checks

- The three law summaries follow Newton's wording rather than silently replacing Law II with the modern special-case formula `F = ma`.
- Universal gravitation is shown as the proportionality `F ∝ m₁m₂/r²`. The modern equation with `G` is explicitly labelled modern notation: Newton did not print that formula or use the constant `G`.
- Book III applies the mathematical work of Books I–II to the system of the world: planetary and lunar motion, tides, precession, and comets.
- The four Rules of Reasoning are compact paraphrases of the 1726 third-edition text.
