2. Part II — There’s More to Morality than Harm and Fairness (chapters 5–8)
3. Part III — Morality Binds and Blinds (chapters 9–12)

## Core claims used in the infographic

- Moral judgment usually begins with fast intuition; conscious reasoning often follows as explanation or advocacy.
- The rider-and-elephant metaphor distinguishes conscious reasoning from the larger set of automatic processes.
- The 2012 book presents six candidate moral foundations: Care/Harm, Fairness/Cheating, Loyalty/Betrayal, Authority/Subversion, Sanctity/Degradation, and Liberty/Oppression.
- Moral Foundations Theory is descriptive, not a declaration that every foundation is morally good.
- Cultures and political groups combine and weight moral foundations differently.
- Groupish capacities enable cooperation, while group loyalty can also narrow perception: morality binds and blinds.
- Reasoning is not useless. Reflection and especially respectful social exchange can trigger new intuitions and improve collective thinking.
