# The Ministry for the Future — V3 integrated infographic research

## Edition and structure

- Kim Stanley Robinson, *The Ministry for the Future*, Orbit, 2020, 576 pages.
- The book has 106 numbered chapters and no author-labeled parts.
- The visual's three chapter modes are explicitly presented as a scholarly reading map: recurring character/ministry chapters; collective or anonymous voices; conceptual, non-human, and expository voices.
- The main narrative spine follows Mary Murphy, director of the Zurich-based Ministry for the Future, and Frank May, survivor of the opening Indian heat catastrophe.

## Core claims represented

- The opening wet-bulb heat catastrophe dramatizes the physical limits of adaptation. Sherwood and Huber (2010) identify 35°C wet-bulb temperature as a theoretical upper limit for sustained human heat dissipation; current empirical research can place practical limits lower, so the poster says “near 35°C” and treats it as a warning threshold rather than a universal forecast.
- The Ministry represents future generations and the biosphere in the spirit of the Paris Agreement.
- The novel's strategy is a portfolio rather than a silver bullet: carbon rewards, central-bank action, glacier stabilization, rewilding, public infrastructure and cooperatives, and climate justice.
- The carbon coin is labeled a speculative proposal in the novel, not a settled policy forecast. Robinson later described it as one example among many possible green financial instruments and emphasized public/fiat money rather than Bitcoin-style cryptocurrency.
