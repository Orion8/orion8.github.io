# 論語 / The Analects — source and accuracy notes

## Edition and authorship frame

The poster treats the work as a layered anthology of sayings, dialogues, and historical anecdotes associated with Confucius and his followers. It does not present the text as a continuous treatise or a verbatim transcript by one author.

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes the conventional transmitted text as twenty chapters and notes that its materials were compiled and stabilized over generations. Columbia University Press likewise describes the work as compiled by disciples in the centuries after Confucius died in 479 BCE.

The poster calls these incipits rather than chapter summaries because the labels come from opening words and do not define a single topic for each book.

## Concept checks

The concept set and synthesis were checked against the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy discussion of ritual psychology, virtues and character formation, and the family and state. Its account identifies five especially central behaviors of the exemplary person: ren 仁, yi 義, li 禮, zhi 智, and xin 信. The poster chooses six recurring reading anchors rather than claiming a canonical fixed list: ren 仁, li 禮, yi 義, xue 學, junzi 君子, and de 德.

The circular model is explicitly labeled as a reading synthesis. Passage locations supporting each move were checked in the Chinese Text Project text: 1.1; 2.1, 2.3, and 2.15; 4.17; 6.30; 12.1, 12.2, and 12.19. The poster does not imply that these passages form one original sequence.

## Visual method

The atmospheric teaching-courtyard illustration was generated without text. The English typography, chapter names, Chinese incipits, passage references, and labels were authored and rendered in a separate SVG layer, then composited into a single 2048 × 3072 WebP poster. The scene is interpretive editorial art, not a claim to reconstruct one historically documented gathering.
