## Accuracy and interpretation

Both images use the received arrangement, not a claim that every early manuscript has 81 identical chapters in this order. Dao and de occur in both parts: the two labels are not exclusive subject compartments. Attribution to Laozi is qualified, not treated as a firmly established single author or exact composition date.

Definitions of Dao, de, ziran and wuwei are concise interpretive glosses, not exhaustive or uncontested translations. De is not simply modern moral virtue; the images emphasize Dao's expression in individual beings. Ziran is self-so/naturalness, not only outdoor scenery. Wuwei is framed as noncoercive action free of excessive desire, not laziness or evasion of responsibility. Four concepts are read together, not offered as an author-stated mechanical causal model. Reading questions are editorial prompts.

Water, vessel and softness are philosophical images relevant to personal life and government, not validated physical laws or universal advice to refrain from intervention. Bamboo supports the softness illustration; chapter 78's actual primary image is water. The cutaway vessel in the English image makes its empty interior visible. No manufacturing design is implied. Illustrated manuscripts use decorative texture, not purported facsimiles or quotations.
