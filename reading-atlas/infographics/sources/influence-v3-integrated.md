# Influence — V3 integrated layout source notes

## Edition baseline

- Robert B. Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*, first published in 1984.
- Poster content follows *Influence, New and Expanded* (2021), which adds Unity as the seventh principle.
- Korean edition: 로버트 치알디니, 황혜숙 옮김, *설득의 심리학 1* 개정증보판 (21세기북스, 2023). Its published contents organize the material into nine parts.

8. Unity / 연대감
9. Instant Influence / 순식간에 설득하라

The official Cialdini sources identify seven universal principles: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity, and unity. Cialdini described six in 1984 and added Unity in the 2021 expanded edition.

## Core claims represented

- People often use reliable mental shortcuts rather than fully analyzing every request.
- Each principle works through a distinct cue: receiving first, prior commitments, similar others, liking, credible expertise, limited availability, or shared identity.
- Effectiveness is conditional. The poster pairs every principle with the situation in which its signal is especially influential.
- The book teaches both ethical application and defense against manipulative or counterfeit uses of the same cues.
- A practical defense loop is: pause, identify the active cue, verify whether the evidence is genuine, and decide from the underlying value rather than the pressure signal.

## Visual logic

The integrated composition is a vertical persuasion workshop. A central decision path runs through seven distinct illustrated mechanisms—not floating cards—into a final ethical checkpoint. Each mechanism combines an object metaphor, a short causal statement, and a defensive question inside one continuous editorial grid.
