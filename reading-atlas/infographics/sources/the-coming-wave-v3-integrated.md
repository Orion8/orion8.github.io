# The Coming Wave — V3 integrated-layout source note

## Edition and Korean title

- Original: *The Coming Wave: Technology, Power, and the Twenty-first Century's Greatest Dilemma* — Mustafa Suleyman with Michael Bhaskar (Crown, 2023).
- Korean edition: *더 커밍 웨이브* — 무스타파 술레이만·마이클 바스카, 이정미 옮김, 한스미디어, 2024. The Reading Atlas card keeps the original title in English; the Korean raster also shows the verified Korean edition title.

## Verified structure

The book has a prologue, four parts, fourteen numbered chapters, and “Life After the Anthropocene.”

1. Part I — Homo Technologicus: “Endless Proliferation”; “The Containment Problem.”
2. Part II — The Next Wave: intelligence, life, the wider wave, four features, and unstoppable incentives.
3. Part III — States of Failure: the grand bargain, fragility amplifiers, the future of nations, and the dilemma.
4. Part IV — Through the Wave: why containment must be possible and ten steps toward it.

## Core argument and models

- AI and synthetic biology form the leading edge of a wider wave amplified by robotics, quantum computing, and advanced energy.
- The **containment problem** is how to preserve benefits while retaining the ability to control, limit, audit, or shut down powerful technologies.
- Four features make the wave unusually hard to govern: **asymmetry, hyper-evolution, omni-use, autonomy**.
- The dilemma is a narrow path between uncontained catastrophe and a surveillance-heavy dystopia.
- Chapter 14 proposes ten concentric, mutually dependent layers: **technical safety, audits, choke points, makers, businesses, government, alliances, culture, movements, coherence**.
- The book treats states as both threatened by the wave and indispensable to containment. Scholarly reviewers value the institutional framing but warn that nation-state-centered solutions need deeper international implementation.

## Reading questions

1. Which parts of the wave can actually be slowed, audited, or shut down—and by whom?
2. When does a protective choke point become an illegitimate concentration of power?
3. Can international coordination move quickly enough without creating permanent emergency governance?
