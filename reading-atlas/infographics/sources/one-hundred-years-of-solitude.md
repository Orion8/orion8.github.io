# Cien años de soledad / One Hundred Years of Solitude — source notes

## Sources checked

- [Instituto Cervantes: *Cien años de soledad*](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/obra/novelas/cien_anos_de_soledad.htm) — circular time, structural symmetry, the recurrence of solitude, Úrsula and other women as continuity, oral tradition, magical realism, social history, and the final deciphering of Melquíades's manuscripts.
- [Penguin: *One Hundred Years of Solitude*](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/482987/one-hundred-years-of-solitude-by-marquez-gabriel-garcia/9780241998502) — the seven generations of the Buendía family; Macondo's wars, disasters, wonders, and miracles; the encoded manuscript; the combination of political reality, fantasy, and comic invention.
- [Penguin Random House: 50th-anniversary Spanish edition](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/196323/cien-anos-de-soledad-50-aniversario--one-hundred-years-of-solitude-by-gabriel-garcia-marquez/) — original title and the family saga's mixture of myth, history, tragedy, rebellion, discovery, and love.
- [Gabriel García Márquez, Nobel Lecture: “The Solitude of Latin America”](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1982/marquez/lecture/) — the historical and political stakes of realities that exceed imported interpretive frameworks.
- [Nobel Prize in Literature 1982](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1982/summary/) — prize motivation describing a richly composed world in which the fantastic and realistic combine to reflect a continent's life and conflicts.
- [Penguin Modern Classics sample](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780241968581/9780241968581-sample.pdf) — publication history and edition check.

## Editorial decisions

- The poster preserves the Spanish original title and uses English-only explanatory text.
- Standard editions present 20 unnumbered, untitled chapters. The six “reading movements” on the poster are therefore explicitly labeled as a reader's map, not divisions supplied by García Márquez.
- The sequence `FOUND → DISCOVER → FIGHT → MODERNIZE → FORGET → DECIPHER` is an interpretive model of Macondo's trajectory, not a quoted formula from the novel.
- Repeated names are presented as a comparison aid rather than a deterministic character key. The novel's repetitions matter because differences and exceptions matter too.
- The poster maps the entire novel and therefore includes the ending.
- The illustrated background was generated without text; all wording and numerals were set in a separate SVG layer for accuracy and legibility.
