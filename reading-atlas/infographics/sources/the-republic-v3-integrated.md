## Accuracy and boundaries

The five groups I / II–IV / V–VII / VIII–IX / X are expressly an editorial reading map of the traditional ten-book arrangement, not formal parts supplied by Plato. The central question asks why one should be just even without the rewards of seeming just; the ring is a motif of the Book II challenge, not an instrument that proves morality.

The city–soul analogy is identified as Plato's argumentative model, not a scientifically established identity. Korean 기개 translates the spirited element (thumos), not spirit as a ghost. Reason's rule and each part's own work are presented as Plato's claim. Critical questions invite scrutiny of hierarchy and political authority, not endorsement of a modern political programme.

The cave sequence is a simplified conceptual illustration, not an optical reconstruction of every object/fire/wall position in the primary allegory. It is kept separate from a quantitative chart and does not claim the four scenes reproduce all stages of the Divided Line. Book VI's Good is included in the structure but not reduced to sunlight as a physical object. The education captions are paraphrases, not direct quotations.
