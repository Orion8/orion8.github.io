# 論語 / The Analects — V3 integrated infographic source notes

## Text and attribution

The Analects is a transmitted collection of brief sayings, dialogues and anecdotes associated with Confucius and his disciples. It was compiled by disciples and later followers over time, rather than written as one continuous treatise by Confucius. The received text comprises **20 books**. The Korean and English posters use “공자와 제자들의 대화 · 후대 제자들이 엮음” / “Conversations of Confucius and his disciples · compiled by later followers” to avoid sole-authorship attribution.

The poster's five numbered sections are an **editorial reading guide**, not the twenty-book text's own divisions. Its “learning and reflection → practicing ren and li → trust and virtuous government” sequence is likewise labeled a *reader's map*: it synthesizes recurring concerns rather than claiming that the book gives a fixed three-stage model.

## Content checks

- **仁 (ren; 인):** humaneness or benevolence; care for others is supported by Analects 12.22 as discussed by the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Neither a complete synonym for modern empathy nor a rule detached from context.
- **禮 (li; 예):** ritual propriety/practice in relationships; Stanford notes that sincere feeling matters alongside the outward performance, especially around Analects 3.4 and 3.26.
- **君子 (junzi; 군자):** the cultivated, exemplary person, not simply a hereditary rank. Learning and repeated conduct shape character.
- **德 (de; 덕):** moral authority/influence of an exemplary ruler, contrasted with mere command; the political reading is a synthesis of discussions across the text, not a single prescribed mechanism.
- Reading questions ask whom an act of humaneness serves and whether ritual includes sincere feeling. These are editorial prompts, not purported verbatim quotations.
