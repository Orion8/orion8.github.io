## Core argument used in the poster

The book replaces sweeping, ideology-first claims about poverty with close observation of how poor households actually decide under severe constraints. Poverty does not have one universal cause or one magic remedy. The useful unit of work is a specific sticking point: identify it, test a plausible intervention, compare outcomes, learn, and revise.

The private-lives chapters examine nutrition, health, schooling, and fertility. The institutions chapters examine risk and insurance, credit and microfinance, saving, entrepreneurship and jobs, and political implementation. The shared visual model is:

`constraint → choice under pressure → feedback → possible trap or escape`

The experimental loop is:

`specific question → random assignment → comparable groups → measured outcome → careful adaptation`

## Five concluding perspectives

1. Information can be missing, confusing, or untrusted.
2. Poor households must make too many high-stakes decisions without reliable services.
3. Markets for insurance, credit, and saving may be absent or badly designed.
4. Institutional and political failures are real, but practical improvements can still be made at the margin.
5. Expectations influence effort and can become self-fulfilling.

These are a reader-oriented synthesis of the book’s conclusion, not extra official parts.

## Reading questions

- What exact constraint, not stereotype, explains the observed choice?
- Is the evidence identifying a real poverty trap or only assuming one?
- Which intervention changes incentives, information, access, or expectations?
- What would need to be retested before transferring a result elsewhere?
