# The Precipice — V3 integrated infographic research notes

## Verified structure

The infographic follows the book's three-part, eight-chapter movement:

1. The Precipice
2. Existential Risk
3. Natural Risks
4. Anthropogenic Risks
5. Future Risks
6. Estimating Risk
7. Protecting the Future
8. Humanity's Potential

The author's official syllabus and FAQ were used to check definitions, risk categories, institutional questions, and longtermism.

## Core claims represented

- Existential risk threatens humanity's long-term potential, not only literal extinction.
- The Precipice begins with the first atomic bomb in 1945: destructive power rose faster than the wisdom and institutions needed to manage it.
- Ord judges anthropogenic risks to exceed natural risks.
- His aggregate estimate for existential catastrophe in the next century is about **one in six**. The graphic explicitly labels this as his personal, approximate degree of belief rather than an established statistical frequency.
- The strategic arc is from understanding and prioritization to international cooperation, safety research, representation of future generations, existential security, and a Long Reflection.

## Approximate risk estimates shown

- All natural risks: 1 in 10,000 per century
- Nuclear war: 1 in 1,000
- Climate change: 1 in 1,000
- Engineered pandemic: 1 in 30
- Unaligned artificial intelligence: 1 in 10
- Aggregate existential risk over the next century: about 1 in 6

These are Ord's estimates, not consensus forecasts.

## Critical perspective

The Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews assessment notes that unprecedented events provide little hard evidence and that rational estimates could differ substantially from Ord's one-in-six figure. The infographic therefore treats the numbers as a framework for disciplined judgment, not settled measurement.

## Reading questions

1. How should low probability and vast harm be weighed together?
2. Which assumption behind the one-in-six estimate is most contestable?
3. How can present institutions represent future generations?
