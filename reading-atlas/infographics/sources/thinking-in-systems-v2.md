# Thinking in Systems — new illustrated bilingual format

## Verified structure

- Introduction: The Systems Lens
- Part 1, System Structure and Behavior: Chapter 1, The Basics; Chapter 2, A Brief Visit to the Systems Zoo
- Part 2, Systems and Us: Chapter 3, Why Systems Work So Well; Chapter 4, Why Systems Surprise Us; Chapter 5, System Traps … and Opportunities
- Part 3, Creating Change—in Systems and in Our Philosophy: Chapter 6, Leverage Points—Places to Intervene in a System; Chapter 7, Living in a World of Systems
- Appendices: System Definitions; Summary of Systems Principles; Springing the System Traps; Places to Intervene in a System; Guidelines for Living in a World of Systems; Model Equations

## Content decisions

- The poster's three-part map follows the Library of Congress table of contents. Shortened panel labels preserve the chapter sequence while fitting the graphic.
- The core stock–flow relation is a reading aid: a stock changes by inflows minus outflows over a time interval.
- The four behavior cards summarize examples developed through the book: reinforcing growth, balancing/goal-seeking behavior, oscillation from delay, and overshoot/collapse.
- The 12 leverage points reproduce Meadows's revised ranking in increasing effectiveness, from parameters (12) to the power to transcend paradigms (1). The poster groups adjacent items into four visual families only to improve readability; it does not alter their rank.
- “Higher numbers are familiar; lower numbers can transform the system” is a concise reading interpretation, not a quotation.
