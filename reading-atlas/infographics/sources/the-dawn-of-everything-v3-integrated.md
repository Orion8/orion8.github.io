# The Dawn of Everything — V3 integrated infographic

Prepared 2026-10-21 for Reading Atlas sequence 26 (catalogue id 20).

## Structure and argument baseline

- The poster follows the book's twelve chapters and groups them into four reader-facing movements: chapters 1–2 challenge conventional origin stories; 3–5 recover political and seasonal variety; 6–8 reconsider farming and cities; 9–12 reconsider the state and the question of why societies became stuck.
- The familiar linear sequence from foraging through farming, cities, states, and inequality is shown as the story the authors challenge, not as an accepted historical law.
- The central model distinguishes three basic freedoms (move, disobey, remake social arrangements) from three forms of domination (sovereignty/control of violence, bureaucracy/control of information, and competitive politics/personal charisma).
- The poster treats claims about flexible cultivation, seasonal political change, and some large cities without kings as the authors' interpretations of debated evidence, not as a new universal ladder.

## Visual and text QA

- Korean and English were composed as two independent, self-contained raster posters.
- Each WebP is 1024 × 1536 and contains every title, label, caption, diagram, and illustration inside the image.
- No HTML text overlay, external font dependency, SVG text, or language-swapping layer is used.
- Full-size output and 390 px-wide raster previews were checked for spelling, chapter ranges, model labels, contrast, and mobile-scale hierarchy.
- The visual path integrates the broken progress ladder, Indigenous–European dialogue, Ice Age and seasonal societies, cultivation, city plans, assemblies, three freedoms, three forms of domination, and multiple possible social routes.
