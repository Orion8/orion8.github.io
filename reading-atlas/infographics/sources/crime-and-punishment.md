# *Преступление и наказание · Crime and Punishment* — source notes

## Edition and structure

- The poster treats *Crime and Punishment* as the 1866 novel and preserves the original Russian title.
- The full Constance Garnett contents at Project Gutenberg confirm six parts followed by an epilogue.
- Chapter counts are Part I, 7; Part II, 7; Part III, 6; Part IV, 6; Part V, 5; Part VI, 8; Epilogue, 2. Total: 41 chapters.
- The short movement names on the poster are interpretive summaries, not replacement titles printed by Dostoevsky.

## Content and interpretation checks

- Oxford University Press describes the central premise as a murder committed on principle by a killer who tries to place himself above society. The poster therefore treats theory as the novel's initiating pressure, not as an accepted conclusion.
- In Part III, Porfiry accurately summarizes the article's division between “ordinary” and “extraordinary” people; Raskolnikov then qualifies the supposed right as an inward permission to cross obstacles when an idea requires it.
- Porfiry's investigation is shown as psychological pressure alongside incomplete positive evidence. This distinction is explicit in Raskolnikov's own reflections after their encounters.
- Sonya's role is described as compassion, faith, presence, and an invitation to confess and accept suffering. The poster does not claim that suffering automatically redeems.
- Svidrigailov is used as a moral mirror and possible trajectory, while Razumikhin represents ordinary solidarity and care. These are reading lenses, not exhaustive allegorical identities.
- The final sentence calls the epilogue the beginning of a possible “new story” of gradual renewal. The poster therefore describes redemption as begun or possible, never completed.
