## Structural baseline

- The 1986 book contains 30 numbered thematic chapters plus an appendix and postscript.
- It is composed as a mosaic of short, cross-connected ideas rather than a single linear derivation.
- The poster groups the official chapter sequence into five editorial reading regions: chapters 1–7, 8–13, 14–17, 18–26, and 27–30. These five regions are reading aids, not named parts in the book.

## Accuracy notes

- The core thesis follows Minsky's official excerpt: a mind can be built from many smaller processes called agents; each agent does something simple, while particular organizations of agents produce intelligence.
- “Agency” is used for organized groups of agents. Conflict, critics, suppressors, and censors are distributed control mechanisms rather than a single executive.
- K-lines reactivate sets of agents associated with an earlier mental state; the poster describes this as reconstructive recall rather than literal storage of a complete snapshot.
- Frames and frame-arrays organize expectations, roles, defaults, and continuity across scenes and language.
- The self is presented as a layered, persistent organization of goals and processes rather than one indivisible inner controller.
