# Don Quijote de la Mancha — poster source notes

The poster preserves Cervantes's Spanish title and uses **Don Quixote** as the English reading title. It treats the modern work as two publication parts: the 1605 volume (52 chapters) and the 1615 continuation (74 chapters), for 126 chapters total.

## Structure checked

- Instituto Cervantes, critical-edition table of contents for the 1605 volume: <https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/clasicos/quijote/edicion/parte1/default.htm>
- Instituto Cervantes, critical-edition table of contents for the 1615 volume: <https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/clasicos/quijote/edicion/parte2/default.htm>
- Instituto Cervantes note on the 1605 volume's original four internal divisions (chapters 1–8, 9–14, 15–27, and 28–52) and its later retrospective designation as Part I: <https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/clasicos/quijote/edicion/parte1/cap52/nota_cap_52.htm>
- Instituto Cervantes introduction on composition: the 1605 volume's episodic form and interpolated stories; the 1615 volume's tighter architecture, reduced use of independent interpolated tales, closer development of knight and squire, and increasing movement toward the end: <https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/clasicos/quijote/introduccion/prologo/rico.htm>

## Interpretive checks

- Penguin Classics describes the novel's experimental form and literary playfulness and notes its foundational place in the modern novel: <https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/286028/don-quixote-by-miguel-de-cervantes-translated-with-an-introduction-and-notes-by-john-d-rutherford/>
- Oxford World's Classics confirms publication in two parts, 1605 and 1615: <https://global.oup.com/academic/product/don-quixote-9780199537891>
- Norton Critical Editions supplies historical contexts and materials on the book's influence across five centuries: <https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393617474>

## Editorial decisions

- The six-step adventure engine is an analytical reading aid, not a chapter scheme.
- Dulcinea is described as an absent/invented ideal rather than an active companion.
- Cide Hamete Benengeli is presented as a fictional historian/source whose mediation makes narrative authority part of the comedy.
- Part II's reader-feedback motif refers to characters who know Don Quixote and Sancho from the printed Part I and to the novel's response to the unauthorized continuation associated with Avellaneda.
- “A foundational modern novel” is used instead of the stronger, contested claim that it is unambiguously the first modern novel.
