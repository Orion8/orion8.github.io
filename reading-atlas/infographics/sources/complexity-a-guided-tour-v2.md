## Structural baseline

The book has five official parts and nineteen numbered chapters:

1. Part One — Background and History (chapters 1–7)
2. Part Two — Life and Evolution in Computers (chapters 8–9)
3. Part Three — Computation Writ Large (chapters 10–14)
4. Part Four — Network Thinking (chapters 15–18)
5. Part Five — Conclusion (chapter 19)

## Accuracy notes

- Complex-systems science seeks common principles behind large collections of components interacting nonlinearly.
- System-level behavior cannot be inferred by merely listing the parts; interactions can produce self-organization, information processing, adaptation, and emergence.
- Simple deterministic rules can generate complex and hard-to-predict behavior when iterated.
- The poster's central loop is an editorial synthesis of recurring mechanisms across the book, not a named equation from the author.
- Four lenses—dynamics, information/computation, evolution, and networks—follow Mitchell's stated foundations for understanding complexity.
