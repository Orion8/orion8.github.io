# The Silk Roads — V3 integrated infographic source note

## Bibliographic baseline

- Peter Frankopan, *The Silk Roads: A New History of the World*.
- First published by Bloomsbury in 2015; the U.S. Knopf edition followed in 2016.
- The full edition contains 25 numbered chapters plus a conclusion.

## Verified structure used in the infographic

The official table of contents was grouped into five readable movements without changing the chapter order:

1. Chapters 1–5: creation of the routes, faiths, the Christian East, revolution, concord.
2. Chapters 6–10: furs, slavery, conquest, Mongol expansion, plague and destruction.
3. Chapters 11–16: gold, silver, northern Europe, empire, crisis and war.
4. Chapters 17–21: oil, compromise, wheat, genocide and Cold War.
5. Chapters 22–25 plus conclusion: American power, superpower rivalry, catastrophe, tragedy and the new Silk Roads.

## Core model and framing

- The “Silk Roads” are treated as a changing network of land and sea corridors, not one road.
- The central visual model is `routes → flows → power`: goods, beliefs, people, armies, pathogens, energy and capital move through the network and reshape political centers.
- “Re-centering,” “exchange and coercion,” and “continuity” are interpretive reading lenses, not neutral facts. The infographic labels them as perspectives to test while reading.
- A scholarly review was used to check the framing: it praises the broad network perspective while noting that any single-volume global synthesis can contain factual errors and sweeping claims.
