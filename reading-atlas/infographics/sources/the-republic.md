# Πολιτεία / The Republic — source notes

## Sources checked

- [MIT Internet Classics Archive: *The Republic*](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.html) — Benjamin Jowett translation with the complete text arranged in the traditional ten-book division.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Plato's Ethics and Politics in *The Republic*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-ethics-politics/) — the challenge posed by Glaucon and Adeimantus, the tripartite soul, justice as psychic order, and the relation between personal and political justice.
- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Ancient Political Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ancient-political/) — the city–soul analogy, four virtues, philosopher-rulers, Kallipolis, and the decline of regimes.
- [Perseus Digital Library: *Republic* 4.433a–443d](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0168%3Abook%3D4%3Asection%3D433a) — justice as each class or part doing its own work, transferred from the city to the soul.
- [Perseus Digital Library: *Republic* 7.514a](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0168%3Abook%3D7%3Asection%3D514a) — the opening of the Cave analogy.
- [Cambridge University Press: *A Plato Primer*, chapter on *The Republic*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/plato-primer/republic/8A71DCAFC366EE41006608AD48AD22B1) — the ten-book convention, Book I's dispute with Thrasymachus, and the work's overarching inquiry into the good life.
- [Cambridge University Press: Proclus, *Commentary on Plato's Republic*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/proclus-commentary-on-platos-republic/38E7D9B214AD4AAA3F2C777E371D204E/listing) — Books V–VII as the central sequence of Sun, Divided Line, and Cave; Books VIII–IX as the decline through timocracy, oligarchy, democracy, and tyranny.

## Editorial decisions

- The poster preserves the Greek title `Πολιτεία` and uses English-only explanatory text.
- The five movement bands are a reader's map of the traditional ten-book arrangement, not divisions supplied by Plato.
- The city–soul table presents Plato's analogy as the argument's organizing model, not as an uncontested proof. “Producers” is a compact label for the productive and money-making class.
- Justice is summarized as ordered cooperation: each part does its own work, reason governs, and the whole becomes unified. Moderation is shown as agreement across the parts that reason should rule.
- The epistemic ascent combines the Sun, Divided Line, and Cave into one reading path. The four line stages are labeled imagination, belief, thought, and understanding; the Cave path is kept visually distinct.
- The regime sequence follows Books VIII–IX: Kallipolis/aristocracy, timocracy, oligarchy, democracy, tyranny. The paired ruling desire is an interpretive reading aid grounded in Plato's city–soul parallel.
- The final questions deliberately surface disputed costs—censorship, hierarchy, guardian communal life, and compelled return to rule—rather than presenting the ideal city as a neutral blueprint.
- The artwork is a code-native SVG infographic made only from geometric shapes, lines, icons, and exact text. It contains no generated or photographic background illustration.
